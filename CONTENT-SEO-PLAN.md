# Content and Semantic SEO Plan

## Purpose and limits

This is a working map for matching each page to a visitor need and to a real MON Events & Bridal service. The intent labels and priorities below are hypotheses based on the site and its enquiry path; they are not keyword-volume or conversion findings. Review them against Search Console and enquiry data after the new domain is live.

The content approach is people-first: provide practical planning help, show MON's actual work and process, and link naturally to the relevant service. Do not publish a venue, service area, credential, price, availability promise, or policy without confirmation from MON Events & Bridal.

### Topic clusters

- **Event styling in Pretoria:** homepage hub → event styling service page → planning guide and gallery → event styling enquiry.
- **Decor hire:** homepage hub → decor hire catalogue → planning guide for scope decisions → hire booking request.
- **Brand and proof:** About page → original portfolio photos and published customer reviews → relevant service page.

The site currently presents event styling and decor hire as the two clear commercial offers. Proposal setups appear in the hire catalogue; do not describe proposal styling as a separate full-service offer unless MON confirms that service.

## Content gaps and priorities

Priorities are based on how directly a content item supports the existing enquiry or booking paths, not on search-volume data.

1. **Wedding decor planning guide — drafted and added.** It answers planning questions and routes readers to the styling enquiry, catalogue, and portfolio. Owner review is still needed before publication.
2. **Real project stories — next.** Add selected project pages or richer portfolio entries with the event type, design decisions, and confirmed venue/location only when MON has verified the details and has permission to publish them.
3. **Decor hire logistics — validate before expanding.** A useful guide could explain reservations, collection/delivery, setup, timing, deposits, and damage terms. Confirm the actual business policies first; do not infer them from form fields.
4. **Event-specific service pages — assess after enquiry review.** Wedding, shower, corporate, and proposal content could be separated if each represents a distinct offer with examples and enough useful detail. Do not create near-duplicate location pages.

## Duplicate and thin-content review

- The homepage and event styling page both address local event decor. The homepage now serves as the broad local hub; the service page targets people comparing full-service wedding and event styling and carries service detail and FAQs.
- The decor catalogue is intentionally product-led. The booking and enquiry forms are transactional utilities and use `noindex, follow`; they are not planned as search landing pages.
- The portfolio is visual proof, not a set of venue or event case studies. Its captions describe visible styling without inventing project names or locations.
- The new guide answers planning questions in more detail than the brief service-page FAQs. Keep future articles distinct by user problem and link them to the appropriate service.
- The About page contains the origin story; the guide links to it for brand context rather than repeating that story.

## Experience, accuracy, and voice

The site has first-party material to build on: MON's published five-step process (Discover, Design, Curate, Create, Celebrate), an origin story on the About page, original portfolio photography, and customer review excerpts on the homepage. Use these as evidence of MON's own approach and work; do not add years of experience, awards, formal credentials, named venues, or guarantees unless supplied and verified.

Draft copy should be warm and direct, with concrete planning details and restrained claims. Prefer clear descriptions of what a visitor can do next over repeated phrases such as “thoughtfully designed” or “something beautiful.” Confirm all operational details, service coverage, and any personal or client story with MON before publishing.
